HOW THE HEIGHT WORKS
====================
--c  the article column: min(780px, 100vw - the theme's clamp() side padding)
--s1 320 to 413px columns   --s2 413 to 577px   --s3 577px and up
--up/--dn  gates: 0 inside a segment's range, huge outside it, so min() picks the
     one segment that applies. Three segments rather than one because the page
     height is a staircase, not a curve: the deck, the source line and the card
     text each re-wrap at their own width, and the two-column card layout starts
     at a 560px column.
Never short at any of the 45 widths measured. Spare averages 44px and peaks at
77px, against 140px+ for a single-line fit.

WHY 577 AND NOT 560
===================
The style runs in the host page, where Windows' classic scrollbars make 100vw
about 17px wider than the real column. A step at 560 would hand a page that is
still in the narrow layout the shorter wide-layout height and clip it. Below a
560px column the guard is only 6px, since a desktop window that narrow is not a
real case and the full 17px would cost phones about 100px of height.

NO GAP UNDER THE FRAME
======================
- `display:block` keeps the iframe off the text baseline, which otherwise leaves a
  few px of descender space beneath it.
- `?fill=1` makes the page stretch to the frame and the card stage take the slack,
  so whatever the formula over-estimates becomes background around the card rather
  than a strip of page showing through.
Checked end to end at 360, 375, 414, 626, 700, 860 and 1280px viewports: no
vertical or horizontal scrollbar, the background meets the frame edge every time,
and the title lines up with the cards.

LINKING TO ONE MOMENT
=====================
Add &step=N (1-7) to open on that card, e.g. ...?fill=1&step=4

AFTER ANY COPY CHANGE
=====================
Re-measure and refit — dev/heights.json holds the measured widths. The deck and the
source line set the height at the narrowest columns, so edit those with care: the
header is inset to the cards now, which makes both wrap sooner than before.
